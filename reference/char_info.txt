Aslan Zırhbaş was an emergency room doctor known for staying calm when others broke. His ability to handle extreme cases and stabilize patients others couldn’t save earned him attention—and eventually, a lucrative offer from a private medical contractor. Promised advanced resources and the chance to work on complex infectious diseases, he accepted, believing he could make a greater impact.

At first, everything seemed legitimate. The facility was well-equipped, the staff professional, and the cases unlike anything he had seen before. Patients arrived with aggressive, fast-acting infections, and Aslan focused on what he did best—keeping them alive. But over time, inconsistencies began to surface. Some patients had no prior records. Others were brought in before showing symptoms. Security was tighter than necessary. Treatment wasn’t focused on recovery, but on observation.

The truth became clear during one case. A restrained patient, not yet fully turned, was brought in. Aslan began treatment immediately, only to receive a direct order: maintain vitals, do not cure, do not terminate—observe. There was no justification. Just repetition. In that moment, he understood. These weren’t patients. They were test subjects, and some had been infected on purpose.

From then on, Aslan quietly prepared to leave. He memorized more than he recorded and gathered what little he could—fragments of infection timelines, early-stage symptoms, and internal reports that contradicted the official story. When his access was suddenly restricted and security began tightening, he knew they were onto him.

He didn’t fight. He adapted. Using his knowledge of the facility, he slipped out before a full lockdown could trap him.

Now, Aslan lives on the move, working as a paramedic while staying off the grid. Umbrella is still looking for him—not just because he left, but because he knows enough to be dangerous. And every time he sees the signs of infection, he’s reminded of the truth he uncovered.
